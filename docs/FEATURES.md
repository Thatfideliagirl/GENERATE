# Generate: every feature, in simple words

This is the complete list of what Generate does. Explain things to the owner of the project in simple words, step by step, because they are not technical. Reply in plain prose, with no bold and no hyphens or dashes.

## What Generate is
Generate is a simple tool for freelancers, brands and small businesses in Nigeria and beyond. It makes the everyday paperwork and the everyday posting. The promise is simplicity: "let's Generate and do it". It does not try to become a big CRM or automation platform.

## Who it is for
Anyone who sells or works for clients: freelancers, small shops, caterers, bakers, hair and beauty, fashion, photographers, designers and consultants. Mostly on phones, often with little data.

## The logo
The name is Generate, written GENErate in the logo. The wordmark has GENE in a heavy style, then "rate" in a lighter style, with a small folded corner document mark inside the G. The logo files are in the public folder. Everything else about the visual style follows the current design of the landing page and interface.

The feel should be premium, warm and human, and never generic or AI looking.

## 1. Documents
1. Invoices, receipts and contracts, each with its own number that counts up.
2. Business details saved once and used on everything: business name, owner, email, phone, address, bank details, logo and signature.
3. Items with quantity and price, a discount, optional VAT and notes.
4. Currency chosen per document (naira, dollar, pound, euro and more). Dashboard totals are grouped by currency.
5. Part payments. Record a deposit now and the balance later. The document shows what has been paid and what is left.
6. A receipt for a payment, created straight from an invoice.
7. Status that works itself out: draft, sent, part paid, paid, overdue, signed.
8. Contracts from starter templates (service agreement, creative project agreement, confidentiality agreement) with fill in places for business, client, owner, date, fee, start and end. They are starting points and not legal advice, so a lawyer should check them.
9. The date fills in automatically from the device clock.
10. Starter items by trade, so a hair stylist or a caterer gets familiar items to tap.
11. Quotes that turn into an invoice in one tap.
12. More document types so Generate is the place for any paperwork: delivery note, purchase order, payment agreement, "I owe you" or loan agreement, offer letter, tenancy agreement, work agreement, NDA and receipt of goods. Each uses the same look system and carries the note that it is not legal advice.
13. Recurring invoices for monthly clients. The person sets the day and Generate prepares the draft and reminds them.

## 2. Looks
1. Six layouts: classic, modern, minimal, framed, split and bold.
2. Six fonts: editorial, classic serif, clean sans, elegant, soft and typewriter.
3. Any accent colour and any text colour.
4. White paper by default, or ivory.
5. A different look for each business, saved on the profile and changeable on each document.
6. A live preview. The same document is used for preview and PDF, so they always match.
7. A template gallery of designs by trade, shown with the person's own logo and name.
8. Letterhead upload. The person uploads their own branded header and footer and Generate fills the middle.

## 3. Signatures
1. Draw a signature with a finger or a mouse.
2. Type a name and choose a handwriting style.
3. Upload a picture of a signature, with the white background removed.
4. Upload any document and place a drawn signature exactly where you want it.
5. Contract signing by link. The owner sends a link, the client opens it on their phone, reads and signs. Both sides get a finished PDF showing the signature, the date and the time. Links use a long random code, can expire and can be cancelled, and a signed document is locked. A lawyer should confirm how Nigerian law treats these signatures before they are advertised as legally binding.

## 4. Sending and getting paid
1. Download a clean PDF, on one page where it fits.
2. Send on WhatsApp with a ready message. Phone numbers starting with 0 get 234 added.
3. Payment reminders on WhatsApp in three tones: friendly, firm and final.
4. A pay page. The client opens the invoice link and sees the amount, the balance and the bank details with a copy button, then taps "I have paid". The owner confirms and records the payment. Card payment through Paystack or Flutterwave can come later. Generate never holds anyone's money.
5. A shareable view link for any document, with the owner's branding, as an alternative to a PDF.

## 5. Everyday money tools
1. Quick Receipt. One screen for sellers: what was sold, the amount, how it was paid, and send on WhatsApp in under ten seconds.
2. Who owes me. A first screen that shows who owes money, late ones first, with one tap reminders and the total owed at the top.
3. A client list with full history, built automatically from documents. Tap a client to start a new document with their details already filled in.
4. A daily nudge, for example "You are owed ₦150,000, two clients are late".
5. A monthly summary: money invoiced, received, still owed, and top clients, with an export as PDF or CSV for tax time.

## 6. Dashboard
1. A greeting with the business name and logo.
2. Totals for money waiting to be paid, money received, overdue invoices and documents saved.
3. A list of all documents with search and filters for invoices, receipts and contracts.

## 7. Account, data and privacy
1. Real accounts with Supabase: email and password, and password reset. Row level security so each person only sees their own data.
2. A profile page to edit business details, look and logo.
3. Business type (freelancer, small business or bigger brand) and trade chosen at setup.
4. Safe backup and sync across devices. Save on the device first and sync when data is available, so work is never lost if the connection drops.
5. Backup and restore as a file.
6. Installable on the phone like an app (PWA), and usable without data after the first visit. Fonts are bundled inside the project and not loaded from Google.
7. An owner admin page showing who signed up: name, business, type, trade, date and email. It must never show anyone's documents or customers.
8. A short privacy page that says so. Nigeria's data protection rules apply.

## 8. Posts: the everyday feature
The reason to open Generate every two days. Full detail is in docs/POSTS_FEATURE.md. In short:
1. A brand brief, filled in once: what they sell, who buys, price range, location, delivery, common customer questions, best sellers, tone and this month's goal.
2. Three ready posts every week using the rule Help, Trust, Act. Help is useful to the customer. Trust removes doubt. Act asks for the order with a real reason. Every post speaks to the customer, not about the business, and has a hook, one idea and one clear next step.
3. Each post is a designed card plus a caption, in the person's logo and look, sized for WhatsApp status and Instagram.
4. An easy price list: type products and prices, pick a style, get a card.
5. Mass cards: paste a list of names, pick a card (thank you, birthday, Sallah, Christmas, new year) and get one personal card per name. Each name has a "tap to send" WhatsApp button. There is no bulk sending, because WhatsApp bans numbers that do it.
6. Customer birthdays, with a weekly reminder to send the card.
7. A calendar of Nigerian occasions, updated every year.
8. A thank you card offered right after an invoice is paid.
9. A reminder to post at a time the person picks. Apps cannot post to WhatsApp status for them.
10. A "this brought an order" tap so Generate learns what works.
11. AI later: "write my caption" and "give me fresh ideas", guided by the brand brief and good examples. It must never invent prices, results or testimonials, and the person always approves the draft. It costs money each time, so it belongs in the paid plan.
12. Start with one trade (cake and bakery), make five or six excellent card designs, test them, then repeat for hair, food, fashion and photography. If the cards look generic, the feature fails.

## Free and Generate More (prices not decided)
Free: invoices, receipts, contracts, quotes, logo, details and signature, all looks, part payments, the dashboard, WhatsApp sending and reminders, upload and sign, any currency, the brand brief, the weekly three posts from the idea library, price list cards, a limited number of mass cards each month, one business profile, and a small "Made with Generate" line on PDFs and cards.
Generate More: backup and sync across devices (decide later whether some of this is free), contract signing by link, the pay page, scheduled reminders, recurring invoices, no Generate line, letterhead and the full template gallery, AI captions and ideas, unlimited mass cards, more than one business profile, monthly summary exports and birthday reminders.
Before prices are set, ask about ten real freelancers and shop owners what they would pay for.

## Rules that apply to everything
1. Simple first. If a screen feels like a big business tool, cut it.
2. Mobile first, fast and light on data.
3. Keep the premium, warm, not AI looking design everywhere. No generic dashboards, no rows of icon cards.
4. Customer names, phone numbers and birthdays are personal information. Keep them private to each user. The admin must never read documents or customers.
5. Keep TypeScript strict and run npm run build before saying a step is done.
6. Test each step with screenshots on a 390 wide phone and a 1280 wide desktop. Look at them and be honest about anything that is not right.
